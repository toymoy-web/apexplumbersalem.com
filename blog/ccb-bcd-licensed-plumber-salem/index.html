CCB vs. BCD: What Oregon's Two-License Plumbing System Means for You

Oregon uses two separate state agencies to license plumbing work: one for the business, one for the individual tradesperson. Here's the real difference and why it matters.

By the Salem Apex Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-04

When you're hiring a plumber in Oregon, you'll likely run across references to CCB licenses and BCD licenses, sometimes on the same invoice or in the same conversation. These aren't two names for the same thing. Oregon actually splits plumbing licensing across two separate state agencies, and understanding what each one covers helps you know what you're actually verifying when you check a contractor's credentials.

The Two Agencies

CCB Covers the Business, BCD Covers the Trade

The Oregon Construction Contractors Board, or CCB, licenses the business itself. A CCB license confirms that a contracting business is registered with the state, carries the required surety bond, and has met the state's business practices requirements. This is the license that shows up when you search a company name on Oregon's contractor lookup tool, and it's the one most homeowners already think to check.

The Oregon Building Codes Division, or BCD, is a separate agency that licenses the individual tradespeople actually performing the plumbing work, through Journeyman Plumber licenses and related trade credentials. This dual structure means a legitimate plumbing business needs both a CCB business registration and BCD-licensed individuals actually doing the work, rather than just one or the other.

Why This Matters to You

A CCB License Alone Doesn't Confirm Trade Competency

Because CCB licensing is about business registration and bonding rather than trade skill verification, a company can hold a valid CCB license without every technician sent to your home necessarily holding an individual BCD trade license, unless you specifically confirm that. This distinction matters most for larger or more technical jobs, where the actual hands-on plumbing work should be performed or directly supervised by someone holding the appropriate BCD credential, not just dispatched from a company with a valid business registration.

This Isn't How Every State Does It

Oregon's System Is Genuinely Different From Some Neighbors

Not every state splits licensing this way. Some states run a single combined licensing system where one credential covers both business registration and trade competency. Oregon's two-agency structure is worth understanding specifically if you've moved from a state with a different system and are used to a single license type covering everything, since the same assumptions don't necessarily transfer to how things work here.

What to Actually Check

Verifying Both Pieces

Oregon's CCB maintains a public license lookup tool where you can verify a business's registration status, bond, and any complaint history. Confirming that a company you're hiring holds an active, good-standing CCB license is a reasonable first step. Beyond that, it's fair to ask directly whether the technician being sent to your property holds an individual BCD Journeyman Plumber license, particularly for anything beyond a simple fixture repair.

All work performed by our team follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and is performed by technicians licensed through Oregon's Building Codes Division, alongside our own CCB business registration. Both pieces matter, and we treat verifying both as a basic standard rather than an extra step.

Why This Framework Exists

Two Different Kinds of Risk

The two-agency system exists because CCB licensing and BCD licensing protect against genuinely different risks. CCB registration and bonding protect consumers financially if a contracting business fails to complete work, doesn't pay subcontractors, or otherwise breaches a contract. BCD trade licensing protects against unsafe or incompetent work by confirming the person actually doing plumbing has demonstrated the required knowledge and hands-on experience. A business can be perfectly legitimate and well-bonded while still, in theory, sending an unqualified person to do the work, which is exactly the gap BCD licensing is meant to close.

The Practical Takeaway

Ask Both Questions, Not Just One

When you're evaluating a plumbing contractor in Salem or anywhere in Oregon, "are you licensed" is really two separate questions: is your business CCB registered, and are your technicians BCD licensed. A contractor who can answer both clearly and confidently is giving you a more complete picture than one who only addresses the business registration side, which is the piece most homeowners already know to ask about.

This two-question habit is worth carrying into any home service hiring decision, not just plumbing. Electrical and HVAC work in Oregon follow related but separately administered licensing structures of their own. The same underlying principle applies: business registration and individual trade competency are genuinely different things, verified by different bodies, and a thorough homeowner checks both rather than assuming one implies the other.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is a CCB license the same as being a licensed plumber?

No. CCB licenses the business and confirms bonding and registration. An individual Journeyman Plumber license comes from the Building Codes Division and confirms trade competency.

How do I check if a business has an active CCB license?

Oregon's CCB maintains a public lookup tool where you can search by business name to verify license status, bonding, and any complaint history.

Do I need to ask about BCD licensing for a small repair?

It's a reasonable question for any job, but it matters most for larger or more technical work where trade competency has more room to make a real difference in the outcome.
