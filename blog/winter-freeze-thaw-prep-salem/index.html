Preparing Salem Pipes for Winter Freeze-Thaw Snaps

Because Salem winters are mild most of the time, homes here often aren't prepared for the occasional genuine cold snap. A little prevention goes a long way.

By the Salem Apex Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-09

Salem's winters are mild by national standards, which is exactly why so many homes here aren't actually prepared for the occasional hard freeze that shows up most years between December and February. A few simple steps before the cold weather arrives can be the difference between an uneventful winter and an emergency call for a burst pipe.

Why Mild Winters Create a Blind Spot

Salem Homes Aren't Built Like Colder-Climate Homes

In genuinely cold climates, pipe insulation, heated crawl spaces, and freeze-resistant plumbing design are standard practice because the alternative simply doesn't survive an average winter. Salem's mild average temperatures mean these measures are less universal here, which is fine most years, but leaves real exposure when a genuine cold snap does arrive. Supply lines running through unheated crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls are the most vulnerable, since they don't benefit from the ambient warmth of the living space above them. A home built decades ago often wasn't designed with this specific risk in mind at all.

The Prep Checklist

What to Actually Do Before the First Hard Freeze

Insulate Exposed Pipes

Foam pipe insulation for lines in crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls.

Disconnect Outdoor Hoses

A connected hose can trap water in the spigot line and cause it to freeze and crack.

Know Your Main Shutoff

Being able to act fast if a pipe does freeze or burst limits the damage significantly.

Seal Gaps Near Pipes

Cold air infiltration through small gaps in foundations or walls accelerates freezing.

During an Actual Cold Snap

Real-Time Steps Worth Taking

Letting a faucet drip slightly on the coldest nights keeps water moving through the line, which makes a complete freeze considerably less likely, since moving water resists freezing far better than standing water. Opening cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls lets warmer room air reach the pipes inside, which matters more than it might seem for a kitchen or bathroom on the coldest side of the house.

Keeping the thermostat at a consistent temperature overnight, rather than turning the heat down significantly to save on energy costs, also matters more during a forecast cold snap than it might during a normal winter night. The energy savings from a lower overnight setting are minor compared to the cost of a burst pipe repair and any resulting water damage.

If a Pipe Does Freeze

Frozen Isn't the Same as Burst, Yet

A pipe that's frozen but hasn't split yet can often be thawed safely, restoring flow before any damage occurs. If a faucet produces no water during a cold snap but there's no visible leak, the line is likely frozen rather than broken. Never use an open flame to thaw a pipe, since uneven, uncontrolled heat is one of the most common ways a simple freeze turns into an actual burst pipe. Our frozen pipe repair team handles safe, controlled thawing specifically to prevent that outcome, using methods that warm the pipe gradually and evenly rather than applying concentrated heat at a single point.

If water is actively flowing from a break, that's a different, more urgent situation. Shut off the main water valve immediately and call our burst pipe repair team right away, since an active burst causes real damage by the minute rather than waiting for a scheduled visit.

Which Homes Are Most at Risk

Not Every Salem Property Faces the Same Exposure

Homes with unheated crawl spaces, especially older construction that predates more recent insulation standards, carry the most risk. Properties in Silverton and other Cascade foothill communities see this even more directly, since the elevation there means more frequent hard freezes and occasional snowfall compared to Salem's own milder valley-floor winters. A homeowner in a colder-prone foothill area should treat pipe insulation as a genuinely higher priority than one living on the immediate Salem valley floor.

The Bottom Line

An Hour of Prep Beats an Emergency Call

The steps that prevent a frozen or burst pipe are genuinely simple: insulate exposed lines, know your shutoff, and take the occasional forecast cold snap seriously even though Salem winters are mild most of the time. The cost of an hour spent on prevention is trivial compared to the cost and disruption of an actual burst pipe and the water damage that often comes with it. Watching the forecast a few days out and prepping ahead of a predicted cold snap, rather than reacting once temperatures have already dropped, is the single most useful habit a Salem homeowner can build around winter plumbing risk.

FAQs

Common Questions

How cold does it actually need to get for Salem pipes to freeze?

Sustained temperatures below freezing for several hours, especially overnight, put exposed pipes at risk, particularly in unheated crawl spaces and garages.

Is dripping a faucet during a cold snap actually effective?

Yes. Moving water resists freezing much better than standing water, and a slight drip is often enough to prevent a full freeze in an exposed line.

How do I know if my pipe is frozen versus already burst?

No water flow with no visible leak usually means frozen. Active water flow or a wet spot means it has already burst and needs immediate attention.
