Local Cause

Why Faucets Wear Out Faster Here

Salem's water comes from the North Santiam River through a large slow-sand filtration system, and while the water quality is generally good, dissolved minerals still travel through the supply and wear down faucet cartridges, O-rings, and valve seats over years of normal use. A faucet that drips, feels stiff to turn, or has lost water pressure is usually showing the effects of that mineral wear rather than a manufacturing defect.

Older homes in Highland, Grant, and South Salem often still have original 1950s and 60s fixtures that have simply outlasted their expected service life. Newer construction in Four Corners and Hayesville tends to see cartridge wear show up a little sooner given the mineral content, just on fixtures installed more recently.

Kitchen sink sprayers and pull-down faucet hoses see this wear even faster than a standard bathroom faucet, since they're used more often and have more moving parts exposed to mineral buildup.

Symptoms

Common Faucet and Sink Problems

Persistent Dripping

Usually a worn cartridge or valve seat that no longer seals fully.

Low Water Pressure at One Fixture

Often mineral buildup restricting flow inside the faucet itself, not the supply line.

Leak Under the Sink

Can come from the faucet base, supply lines, or the sink drain assembly.

Stiff or Difficult Handle

Mineral buildup inside the cartridge making the handle harder to turn smoothly.

Repair or Replace

When a New Faucet Makes More Sense

A worn cartridge, O-ring, or valve seat is usually a straightforward repair on most faucet brands, and parts are commonly available for popular models. An older faucet with a discontinued cartridge, visible corrosion at the base, or a finish that's failing is a different situation where replacement is often the more practical choice, especially if you're already updating the sink or countertop.

What We Check

A Full Sink Inspection, Not Just the Faucet

When we're called for a leak under a sink, we check the faucet, the supply lines, the P-trap, and the sink drain assembly together. A leak that looks like it's coming from the faucet sometimes actually originates at a loose supply line connection or a failing P-trap gasket instead. Cabinet flooring and shelving underneath a sink often shows the earliest signs of a slow leak before the leak itself becomes obvious, so we check for soft or discolored wood as part of the same visit.

Prevention

Reducing Mineral Wear Between Repairs

Wiping down faucets regularly prevents mineral deposits from hardening on the finish and around the base. A whole-house water softener addresses the root cause rather than just the symptom, since it reduces the calcium and magnesium content that wears down cartridges and valve seats throughout the home, not just at one faucet. For households that see repeat faucet issues across multiple fixtures, that's often the more cost-effective long-term fix.

All repair and installation work follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), by technicians licensed through Oregon's Building Codes Division.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my faucet keep dripping even after I tighten it?

A drip usually comes from a worn cartridge or valve seat inside the faucet, not a loose connection. Tightening the handle won't fix worn internal parts.

Can you fix any faucet brand?

We service most major brands. For an older or discontinued model where parts aren't available, replacement is sometimes the more practical option.

Why is my kitchen faucet losing pressure?

Mineral buildup inside the faucet aerator or cartridge is the most common cause, especially on faucets that see heavy daily use.

Do you replace supply lines too?

Yes, we check supply lines during any sink repair and replace them if they're corroded or the source of the leak.
