Distinct From Burst Pipe Repair

Frozen, Not Yet Burst

A frozen pipe that hasn't split yet is a different situation from an active burst, and it's worth treating differently. If water isn't flowing from a fixture but nothing is actively leaking, the line may simply be frozen rather than broken, and thawing it carefully can prevent it from ever bursting at all. If a pipe has already split and water is flowing, that's an active emergency and our burst pipe repair team should be your first call instead.

West Salem's slightly higher elevation and hillier terrain means exposed crawl space and garage lines there can run a few degrees colder than comparable spots on the valley floor during a hard freeze. That's worth factoring in if your home sits on the west side of the river.

Local Cause

Why Pipes Freeze in Salem's Mild Winters

Salem's winters are milder than much of the country, which is exactly why the occasional hard freeze catches homes off guard. Supply lines running through unheated crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls don't get the ambient warmth of the living space above them. A short but genuine cold snap between December and February, which happens most years, is enough to freeze an exposed line that was never insulated for real winter conditions.

Signs

Signs a Pipe Is Frozen

No Water From a Fixture

A tap that produces nothing, or just a trickle, during cold weather.

Frost on Visible Pipe

Visible frost on an exposed line in a crawl space or garage.

Bulging Section of Pipe

Ice expansion can visibly bulge a pipe before it splits.

Unusual Odor From a Drain

A frozen drain trap can sometimes push sewer odor back into the home.

Safe Thawing

How We Thaw a Frozen Pipe

1

Locate

Find the frozen section, often where the pipe is least insulated.

2

Shut Off if Needed

Close the local shutoff if there's any sign of a crack forming.

3

Thaw Gradually

Controlled heat application, never an open flame or uncontrolled heat source.

4

Inspect

Check the thawed section for hairline cracks before restoring full flow.

What Not to Do

Never use an open flame to thaw a pipe yourself. Uneven, uncontrolled heat is one of the most common ways a simple freeze turns into a burst pipe and a fire risk at the same time. All repair work follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Prevention

Protecting Pipes Before the Next Freeze

Insulating exposed pipes in crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls before winter is the most effective single step. Letting a faucet drip slightly on the coldest nights keeps water moving through the line, making a full freeze much less likely. Knowing where your main shutoff valve is located means you're ready if a pipe does split despite precautions.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if my pipe is frozen versus actually burst?

No water flow with no visible leak usually means frozen. Active water flow or a wet spot means it has already burst, which needs immediate emergency response.

Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

Carefully, with gentle heat like a hair dryer on low, yes. Never use an open flame or a high-heat source, which can crack the pipe or start a fire.

Why does Salem even have freeze problems if winters are mild?

Mild average winters mean less pipe insulation than in colder climates, so the occasional genuine cold snap between December and February catches exposed lines unprepared.

Should I leave a faucet dripping during a cold snap?

Yes, a slight drip keeps water moving through the line and makes a full freeze much less likely.
