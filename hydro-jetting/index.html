Keeps Clogging?

If a Snake Cleared It But It Backed Up Again, This Is Why

If a plumber already snaked your line and it clogged again within a few weeks, or the cable got stuck partway through, that's usually a sign of root intrusion, not a one-time blockage. A snake punches a channel through whatever's in the way, but it doesn't touch the buildup coating the rest of the pipe. In Salem's clay soil, that leftover film gives new root growth and sediment something to grab onto almost immediately, and the same call repeats every few months. Hydro jetting is the fix built specifically for that pattern.

If a video inspection already found root intrusion or heavy sediment, jetting is the fix built for that.

Local Cause

Why Root Intrusion Is So Common Here

Mature Oregon white oaks and Douglas firs line the streets in older parts of Salem, including Highland, Grant, South Salem, and Morningside. Their root systems actively search out moisture, and a hairline crack or loose joint in an aging clay or cast iron sewer lateral is exactly the kind of moisture source they find. Once roots get a foothold inside the pipe, they branch out into a dense mass that traps grease, paper, and clay sediment washed in by Salem's winter rains.

The rainy season from October through March makes this worse, since saturated soil swells and shifts around buried pipes, opening up new joint gaps for roots to exploit right as root growth is also active. West Salem, Lancaster, and older stretches of the Salem Hills carry the same risk profile wherever original clay tile or cast iron laterals are still in the ground.

Commercial Properties

Hydro Jetting for Grease-Heavy Commercial Lines

Restaurants and food service properties along Commercial Street and other older business corridors deal with a different version of the same problem. Cooking grease solidifies inside kitchen drain lines and grease traps far faster than a typical residential clog, and cable snaking alone rarely clears it for long. Routine hydro jetting on a scheduled basis is standard practice for keeping commercial kitchen lines and grease traps code-compliant and backup-free.

How It Works

What Actually Happens During Hydro Jetting

We start with a video camera inspection to confirm the pipe is in good enough structural condition for jetting and to see exactly where the root mass or sediment buildup sits. A specialized hose then feeds a nozzle down the line that fires water at roughly 3,000 to 4,000 PSI in a pattern that scours the entire interior pipe wall, not just the blocked section. A second camera pass confirms the line is fully clear before we leave.

When It's the Right Fix

Hydro Jetting vs. a Standard Snake

Cable Snake

  • Fast for one isolated clog
  • Lower cost per visit
  • Leaves pipe-wall buildup in place
  • Poor fit for root intrusion

Hydro Jetting

  • Clears root mass and sediment fully
  • Cleans the whole pipe wall
  • Best fit for recurring or main-line clogs
  • Requires a camera check first for pipe condition

Jetting isn't the right call for every pipe. A line with a hairline crack or heavy corrosion can be damaged further by high-pressure water, which is exactly why we camera-check first rather than jetting blind. If the pipe's condition rules jetting out, we'll walk you through sewer line repair instead.

Response Time

Emergency or Scheduled Service

A slow drain that's been getting gradually worse over a week or two can usually wait for a same-day or next-day jetting appointment once we've camera-checked the line. A sewer line that's completely blocked and backing up into the home is different, and we treat that as an emergency dispatch regardless of the hour. Maintenance

How Often Should a Line Be Jetted

A line with no history of root intrusion or heavy sediment usually doesn't need jetting on any fixed schedule, we'll only recommend it when a camera inspection shows a real reason. For a property that's already had root intrusion once, an annual or biannual preventive jetting service is typically the cheaper path over several years. That's because roots keep growing back into the same crack whether or not the line has clogged yet.

FAQs

Common Questions

The plumber snaked my line and it clogged again a few weeks later. Why?

A cable snake clears a path through the blockage but leaves grease film, clay sediment, and root hairs coating the rest of the pipe, giving a new clog somewhere to grab onto quickly.

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

We run a camera inspection first. Jetting is safe for most cast iron, clay, and PVC lines in good shape, but not for a pipe that's already cracked or badly deteriorated.

How is hydro jetting different from a regular drain snake?

A snake mechanically punches through a clog. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water, typically 3,000 to 4,000 PSI, to scour the entire pipe wall.

Do you offer hydro jetting as an emergency service?

Yes, when a line is fully blocked and backing up into the home. Otherwise we typically schedule it the same or next day.

Can hydro jetting damage my pipes?

Jetting is safe for pipe in sound structural condition, which is exactly why we camera-check first. It's not the right call for a pipe that's already cracked, badly corroded, or has collapsed sections.

Do you jet commercial grease traps and kitchen lines?

Yes. Restaurants and food service properties along Commercial Street and similar corridors typically need routine jetting to keep grease-heavy lines and traps code-compliant.
