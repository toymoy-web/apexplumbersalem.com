Why Dallas Homes Call Us

The Plumbing Problems We See Most in Dallas

Dallas sits at the base of the Eola Hills, on the same Jory soil formation that underlies much of the Willamette Valley's hill country, including the Salem Hills to the east. That clay-heavy soil expands and contracts seasonally the same way it does closer to Salem, shifting buried sewer lines and stressing older foundations, but Dallas homes also deal with the added factor of hillier terrain, which affects drainage patterns differently than the flat valley floor.

As the Polk County seat since the city's 1874 incorporation, Dallas has a genuine mix of older homes near the historic downtown and Polk County Courthouse, alongside newer residential growth farther from the center. That mix means we see a wider range of pipe materials and ages on a given day here than in a more uniformly-aged neighborhood.

Dallas was originally platted as a town called Cynthian before being renamed in 1852, and it grew through the late 19th and early 20th centuries on a hops and berry farming economy. That agricultural history still shows up in the land use patterns around the city today, with working farms bordering residential growth in several directions. The city's namesake, incidentally, is unrelated to the Texas city of the same name.

Diagnosis

What We Check First on a Sloped Dallas Property

For a Dallas home dealing with recurring drain or sewer issues, we pay particular attention to how the property sits relative to the surrounding grade before assuming the cause is identical to a flat-lot home. A sewer line running downhill toward the connection point settles differently over time than one running level, and that difference matters for diagnosing a bellied section versus straightforward root intrusion. All repair work follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), regardless of a property's slope or age.

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

Dallas's hillier terrain and clay-heavy soil mean a burst pipe or active leak can move fast, especially on a sloped property. Here's what to do while you wait for us.

1

Shut Off the Main Valve

Stops the flow of water immediately and limits how much damage occurs before help arrives.

2

Kill Power to Wet Areas

If water is near outlets or fixtures, shut off power to that area at the breaker if it's safe to reach.

3

Clear the Immediate Area

Move furniture, electronics, and belongings away from standing or spreading water.

4

Call Our 24/7 Line
