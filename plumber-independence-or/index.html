Historic District

The Independence Historic District Shapes Our Approach Here

Thirty square blocks of Independence's oldest section are listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Independence Historic District, and that designation reflects genuinely old building stock along the west bank of the Willamette River. Plumbing in and around the historic district often means working with original construction from well before modern code, which calls for a more careful diagnostic approach than a straightforward newer-home repair. That combination of age and preservation status is genuinely different from most of the towns we serve, where original construction from this far back is far less common.

Outside the historic core, Independence's more recent residential growth follows patterns similar to the rest of the Salem metro area, with 20th-century era pipe materials and fixtures that are more standard to diagnose and service.

The city was founded by pioneers with ties to Independence, Missouri, and grew steadily along the river into the well-preserved downtown that earned its historic district designation. Independence was once known as the "Hop Capital of the World," and much of the historic downtown dates directly to that era of prosperity.

Diagnosis

What We Check First in a Historic District Home

For a property inside or near the historic district, we start by identifying what original materials are actually still in place before touching anything. A well-preserved historic home sometimes has a mix of original and previously-updated plumbing that isn't obvious without opening a wall or checking under a fixture. Getting that picture right the first time avoids surprises partway through a repair. A camera inspection on a sewer or drain line is often worth the extra step here specifically because assumptions based on the home's age alone can be wrong in either direction.

That diagnostic step matters for cost as much as accuracy. Quoting a repair before confirming what pipe material is actually behind a wall risks a mid-job change order once the real condition is visible. A homeowner in a historic-district property deserves an upfront number that already accounts for what we're likely to find, not a low estimate that grows once work begins.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

Independence's historic district homes often have original plumbing behind the walls, which means a burst pipe can be harder to isolate quickly. Here's what to do while you wait for us.

1

Shut Off the Main Valve

Stops the flow of water immediately and limits how much damage occurs before help arrives.

2

Kill Power to Wet Areas

If water is near outlets or fixtures, shut off power to that area at the breaker if it's safe to reach.

3

Clear the Immediate Area

Move furniture, electronics, and belongings away from standing or spreading water.

4

Call Our 24/7 Line
