Local Cause

When a Line Is Too Damaged to Repair

Salem's clay soil movement and mature-tree root intrusion don't just crack pipes, they can deteriorate an entire lateral over decades. Homes still running original clay tile or Orangeburg pipe, a wood-pulp and coal-tar material common from the 1940s through the early 1970s, sometimes reach a point where the pipe has softened or collapsed along most of its length rather than at one isolated spot. At that point, patching one section only delays the next failure a few feet down the line.

West Salem, Lancaster, and pockets of the Salem Hills carry the same risk wherever original clay laterals are still in the ground and were never upgraded during a remodel.

Replacement Methods

Trenchless Pipe Bursting vs. Open-Cut Replacement

Trenchless pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the path of the old one while breaking the old pipe apart ahead of it, which replaces the full line without trenching your entire yard. It works well when the ground above the line is otherwise undisturbed and there's a clear path for the new pipe to follow. Open-cut replacement, digging a trench along the full length of the line, is sometimes the more practical choice when the old line has shifted significantly out of its original position or when other underground utilities are in the way.

We camera-inspect and map the line before recommending either method, and we'll explain honestly which one fits your yard rather than defaulting to whichever is easier for us. All work is performed to the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), by technicians licensed through Oregon's Building Codes Division.

If Sewage Has Backed Up

Safety Before We Arrive

If sewage has backed up while you're waiting for line replacement to be scheduled, keep children and pets away from the affected area and avoid direct contact with the water. Ventilate the area if possible, and don't run water in fixtures connected to the failing line until we've assessed it.

After Replacement

What to Expect Afterward

A new sewer lateral, whether installed by trenchless bursting or open-cut, is typically modern PVC that won't suffer the same clay-soil movement or root intrusion problems as the original clay tile or Orangeburg pipe. That doesn't mean the line is immune to every future issue. Grease and hair clogs can still happen the same as with any line, they'll just be routine drain maintenance rather than a structural failure.

If open-cut excavation was used, expect some settling in the backfilled trench over the following months as the soil compacts. That's normal and doesn't indicate a problem with the new line itself.

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does a sewer line replacement take?

A trenchless replacement often takes one to two days. Open-cut excavation can take longer depending on line length and obstacles along the path.

Will replacing my sewer line tear up my landscaping?

Trenchless pipe bursting disturbs much less surface area than open-cut excavation. We'll tell you honestly which method fits your yard before work begins.

Do I need a permit for sewer line replacement?

Yes, and we handle the permitting and final inspection as part of the project.

Is sewer line replacement covered by insurance?

Usually not, unless a specific sudden event caused the failure. Gradual deterioration from age or root intrusion typically isn't covered.
