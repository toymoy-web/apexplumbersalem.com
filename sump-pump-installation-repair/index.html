Local Cause

Why Sump Pumps Matter in Salem

Marion County receives roughly 40 inches of rain a year, most of it between October and March, and homes with crawl space or basement construction are especially vulnerable to groundwater intrusion during sustained wet stretches. A sump pump that's undersized, aging, or simply not installed at all is one of the more preventable causes of water damage we see during Salem's wettest months.

Signs

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention

Running Constantly

A pump that never seems to stop may be undersized for your groundwater conditions.

Not Turning On During Heavy Rain

A float switch or check valve failure can leave the pump inactive when it's needed most.

Loud or Unusual Noise

Grinding or rattling often signals a failing motor or debris in the pump housing.

Visible Moisture Despite Having a Pump

Can mean the pump isn't keeping up with actual water volume.

Heavy Rain in the Forecast?

We can inspect or install a sump pump before the next storm.

Installation

Sizing a System Correctly

1

Assess

Check crawl space or basement conditions and groundwater patterns.

2

Size the Pump

Match pump capacity to actual water volume, not a generic default.

3

Install

Set the pump, basin, and discharge line properly routed away from the foundation.

4

Test

Run water through the system to confirm proper activation and discharge.

We also install battery backup systems for households concerned about power outages during major storms, since a sump pump that loses power at the worst possible moment defeats the purpose of having one at all.

Repair

Common Sump Pump Problems

A stuck float switch, a clogged check valve, or a failing motor are the most common repair calls. We diagnose which component has failed before recommending a repair or a full pump replacement, since an aging pump nearing the end of its service life sometimes makes replacement the more reliable choice even when a specific part could technically be fixed.

Maintenance

Keeping a Sump Pump Ready

Testing a sump pump before the rainy season starts, by pouring water into the basin and confirming it activates and discharges properly, catches a failed float switch or stuck check valve before it matters during an actual storm. Clearing debris from the basin periodically prevents the kind of clog that causes a pump to run constantly without actually moving water efficiently.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I really need a sump pump in Salem?

If your home has a crawl space or basement prone to groundwater intrusion during the rainy season, yes. It's one of the more preventable causes of water damage we see.

What happens if the power goes out during a storm?

A standard sump pump stops working without power. A battery backup system keeps it running during an outage, which is worth considering for major storm events.

How long do sump pumps typically last?

Most pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. Constant running or age near that range are both reasons to consider replacement.

Can you install a sump pump in an existing crawl space?

Yes, we assess existing conditions and install a properly sized system with correctly routed discharge, even in homes that never had one before.
