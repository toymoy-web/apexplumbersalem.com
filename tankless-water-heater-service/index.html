Distinct From Repair or Replacement

Servicing a Tankless Unit Specifically

Tankless water heaters fail differently than a standard tank, and they need their own maintenance schedule to run reliably. Our water heater repair team handles a tankless unit that's stopped working, and our installation and replacement team handles a new tankless install. This page covers what makes tankless systems different: the descaling maintenance they need and the diagnostic approach that catches problems before they become a full breakdown.

Local Cause

Why Tankless Units Need Descaling in Salem

Salem's water comes from the North Santiam River through a large slow-sand filtration system, and while the water quality is generally good, dissolved minerals still pass through the treatment process. In a standard tank, those minerals settle as sediment at the bottom. In a tankless unit, they scale onto the heat exchanger instead, gradually restricting flow and reducing heating efficiency. Without regular descaling, a tankless unit can lose a meaningful share of its efficiency within just a few years.

Signs

Signs a Tankless Unit Needs Service

Reduced Hot Water Flow

Scale buildup on the heat exchanger restricting water flow through the unit.

Inconsistent Temperature

Fluctuating heat output can point to scale or a failing flow sensor.

Error Codes on the Display

Most tankless units display a specific code that helps diagnose the issue quickly.

Longer Wait for Hot Water

Can indicate the unit is struggling to reach temperature efficiently.

Descaling Process

How Descaling Works

1

Isolate

Shut off and isolate the unit using its service valves.

2

Flush

Circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger.

3

Rinse

Clear the solution and flush the system with clean water.

4

Test

Restore service and confirm flow rate and temperature are back to normal.

Most manufacturers recommend annual descaling for homes on municipal water, and this service is straightforward if a unit was installed with the isolation valves needed to do it properly. If your unit doesn't have those valves, we can add them during a service visit so future descaling is faster and less disruptive.

Diagnostics

Reading Error Codes

Tankless units display manufacturer-specific error codes rather than failing silently, which actually makes diagnosis more precise than with a standard tank. We check the code against the manufacturer's documentation before assuming a repair is needed, since some codes point to something as simple as a venting obstruction rather than a failing component inside the unit itself.

FAQs

Common Questions

How often should a tankless water heater be descaled?

Most manufacturers recommend annually for homes on municipal water. Skipping descaling is one of the most common reasons a tankless unit underperforms or fails early.

My tankless unit is showing an error code. What does that mean?

Each manufacturer uses specific codes for specific issues. We check the code against the unit's documentation to diagnose the actual cause rather than guessing.

Can you add isolation valves to my existing unit?

Yes, if your unit wasn't installed with the valves needed for descaling, we can add them during a service visit.

Is a tankless unit worth the extra maintenance compared to a tank?

For most households, yes. Annual descaling is a small, predictable cost compared to a tank's eventual full replacement, and tankless units typically last longer overall.
