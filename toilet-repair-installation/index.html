Local Cause

Why Toilets Need Service So Often Here

Salem's municipal water carries dissolved minerals from the North Santiam River supply, and over years those minerals build up inside a toilet's fill valve and flush valve, causing running toilets, weak flushes, and fill valves that won't fully shut off. Homes with original toilets from before Oregon's low-flow fixture requirements took effect also tend to have older internal components that are simply due for replacement regardless of water quality.

This shows up often in the older housing stock of Highland, Grant, Morningside, and South Salem, where original fixtures from well before the low-flow requirement are still common. Newer builds in Four Corners and Hayesville more often see mineral buildup as the main issue rather than genuinely outdated hardware.

Symptoms

Common Toilet Problems We Fix

Running Continuously

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve that isn't sealing properly.

Weak or Incomplete Flush

Can be a clogged jet, a low water level, or an older, inefficient toilet design.

Rocking or Leaking at the Base

Often a worn wax ring, which can lead to water damage on the floor below if left unaddressed.

Frequent Clogging

Can point to a partial drain blockage rather than the toilet itself.

Repair or Replace

When Replacement Makes More Sense

A worn flapper, fill valve, or wax ring is a straightforward, lower-cost repair on most toilets. A cracked tank or bowl, a toilet that needs frequent repairs, or an older high-water-use model are different situations where full replacement often pays for itself through fewer repair calls and lower water use going forward.

Installation

New Toilet Installation

We install standard two-piece toilets, one-piece models, and comfort-height options, properly sealed with a new wax ring and shut-off valve check as part of every installation. All new installations meet current water-efficiency standards, which also tends to lower your water bill compared to an older high-flow model.

We also check the condition of the existing shut-off valve and supply line during installation, since a corroded valve is a common source of a slow leak that gets blamed on the new toilet when it's actually unrelated hardware. Replacing a worn valve at the same time as the toilet avoids a second service call down the road. If you're updating the toilet as part of a larger bathroom refresh, it's often worth also looking at shower and bathtub installation while the space is already being worked on.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my toilet keep running after I flush?

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve that isn't sealing properly. It's typically a quick, low-cost repair.

Is a rocking toilet a serious problem?

It can be. A rocking toilet often means the wax ring seal has failed, which can let water leak onto the floor below and cause damage if it goes unaddressed.

How long does a new toilet installation take?

Most installations are completed within an hour or two, including removal and disposal of the old unit.

Are all new toilets low-flow?

Yes, current fixtures sold and installed meet water-efficiency standards, which also tends to reduce your water bill compared to an older model.
