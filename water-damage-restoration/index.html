Distinct From Plumbing Repair

Restoration, Not Just the Pipe Repair

Water damage restoration is a different service from the plumbing repair that stopped the leak. If a burst pipe or sewer backup has already soaked flooring, drywall, or belongings, our burst pipe repair or sewer line repair teams handle the plumbing fix, while this restoration service handles the water extraction, structural drying, and cleanup that comes after.

Local Cause

Why Water Damage Happens So Often in Salem

Marion County receives roughly 40 inches of rain a year, most of it between October and March, and the Willamette River and its tributary creeks, including Mill Creek, Pringle Creek, and Croisan Creek, have a documented history of overflowing during heavy rain years. Beyond flooding, Salem's volcanic clay soil movement stresses buried plumbing lines, and older homes with crawl space construction are especially vulnerable to moisture intrusion during sustained wet stretches.

Our Process

Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers dry structural materials.

Our process follows IICRC S500 water damage restoration guidelines, the published industry standard for water damage response, covering everything from initial assessment through structural drying verification.

Documentation

What to Document Before We Arrive

If it's safe to do so, take photos or video of the affected area and any damaged belongings before cleanup begins. This documentation is often useful if you file a claim with your insurance company, since it captures the extent of damage before drying and cleanup change what's visible. We're not a substitute for your insurance adjuster, but a clear record from the start tends to make that process smoother.

Scope of Service

What This Service Covers

Water damage restoration covers the cleanup and drying side of a water event: extracting standing water, drying out saturated flooring and drywall, and addressing early mold risk before it becomes a bigger problem. It's a distinct service category from plumbing repair, which covers fixing the pipe, fixture, or line that caused the water in the first place. A burst pipe is a plumbing repair job, and the water it left behind is a restoration job, even though both happened from the same event.

The urgent, first-response phase of that cleanup, extraction and drying within the first 24 to 48 hours, is what our water mitigation service covers specifically. Restoration picks up from there with the fuller repair and rebuild once the property is stable.

This distinction matters for how you get help fastest: if the leak is still active, stopping it comes first, which is why our burst pipe repair and sewer line repair teams often coordinate directly with the restoration crew rather than working the two jobs separately.

FAQs

Common Questions

How fast do you respond to water damage calls?

We treat active water damage as an emergency and dispatch as quickly as we can, since the first 24 to 48 hours matter most for preventing mold growth and further structural damage.

Do you handle the insurance claim for me?

We're not your insurance company, but we document the damage thoroughly and can provide the records your adjuster needs. Taking your own photos before we start is a good idea too.

Is water damage restoration covered by insurance?

Sudden, accidental water events are often covered, while gradual damage from long-term neglect typically isn't. Check your specific policy for the details.

Do you also fix the plumbing problem that caused the damage?

Water damage restoration and the plumbing repair are separate services. Depending on the cause, our burst pipe repair or sewer line repair teams handle the plumbing fix directly.
