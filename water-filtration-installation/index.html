Distinct From Softening

Filtration Targets Different Problems Than a Softener

Water filtration and water softening solve different problems, even though people sometimes ask for one when they mean the other. A softener removes calcium and magnesium hardness minerals. A filtration system targets chlorine taste and odor, sediment, and in some cases specific contaminants, without necessarily addressing hardness at all. Some Salem households want both systems working together, one for hardness and one for taste and sediment.

If you're only dealing with one issue, taste and odor without any hardness symptoms, or the reverse, there's no need to install both systems just because they're often sold together. We'll recommend based on what your water test actually shows.

Local Cause

Why Salem Homeowners Add Filtration

Salem's water is treated through one of the largest slow-sand filtration systems in the country and is generally safe to drink straight from the tap, but municipal treatment includes chlorine for disinfection, which some households notice in taste or smell. Homes on private wells outside city limits sometimes deal with sediment or iron content that municipal customers don't encounter at all, which changes the right filtration approach.

System Types

Whole-House vs. Point-of-Use Filtration

Whole-House Filtration

Installed where water enters the home, filtering chlorine and sediment from every tap.

Under-Sink Drinking Water Systems

Targeted filtration for the kitchen tap, often including more thorough contaminant reduction.

Well Water Systems

Sediment and iron filtration sized to a private well's specific water quality.

Combined Systems

Whole-house filtration paired with a dedicated drinking water system for the kitchen.

Reverse Osmosis

The most thorough under-sink option for households wanting the highest level of contaminant reduction.

Filter Media

What's Actually Inside a Filter

Most whole-house and under-sink systems use activated carbon to reduce chlorine taste and odor, sometimes paired with a sediment pre-filter that catches particulate matter before it reaches the carbon stage. Well water systems often add iron or manganese reduction media specific to what a water test finds, since sediment and mineral content vary significantly from one well to the next even within the same neighborhood.

All installation work follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), by technicians licensed through Oregon's Building Codes Division.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need a softener, a filtration system, or both?

It depends on what's actually in your water. A softener addresses hardness minerals. Filtration addresses chlorine, sediment, and taste. A water test tells us which one, or both, actually fits your situation.

Is Salem tap water safe without filtration?

Yes, municipal water meets safety standards and is treated through a large slow-sand filtration system. Filtration at home is about taste, sediment, and preference, not safety in most cases.

How often do filters need to be replaced?

It varies by system and household water use, typically every 3 to 12 months for most cartridge-based filters.

Do well water homes need different filtration than city water homes?

Often yes. Private wells can have sediment or iron content that municipal water doesn't, which changes the right filtration approach.
