Local Cause

When Repair No Longer Makes Sense

Salem's water comes from the North Santiam River through a large slow-sand filtration system, and while the water itself is generally clean, dissolved minerals still accumulate inside a tank over years of use. Once a tank starts leaking, shows heavy internal corrosion, or passes the 10 to 12 year mark, replacement is almost always the better long-term value even though the upfront cost is higher than a repair.

City of Salem building code requires a permit for water heater replacements, and our technicians handle that process as part of the installation so you're not left tracking down paperwork after the fact.

Signs

Signs You Need Replacement, Not Repair

Visible Tank Leak

Moisture pooling at the base of the tank means the tank itself has failed.

Rusty Water

Discolored hot water often signals internal corrosion that repair can't reverse.

Age Past 10 to 12 Years

Tanks in this range are more likely to fail again soon after a repair.

Tank vs. Tankless

Choosing a Replacement Unit

A standard tank unit costs less upfront and installs faster, which makes sense for most households replacing a failed tank on a like-for-like basis. A tankless unit costs more to install, partly because venting and gas line sizing often need upgrading, but it delivers continuous hot water and typically lasts longer since there's no tank to corrode.

Traditional Tank

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Faster, simpler installation
  • Limited hot water supply
  • 10 to 12 year typical lifespan

Tankless

  • Continuous hot water on demand
  • Lower monthly energy cost over time
  • Compact wall-mounted unit
  • 15 to 20 year typical lifespan

If you're leaning toward tankless, our tankless water heater service team can also flush and descale an existing unit if replacement turns out not to be necessary yet.

After Installation

Getting the Most Out of a New Unit

A new tank benefits from the same mineral-buildup protection older units need. An annual flush keeps sediment from Salem's water supply from insulating the heating element early in the unit's life, which is the single easiest way to actually hit that 10 to 12 year lifespan instead of falling short of it. For a new tankless unit, an annual descaling service protects the heat exchanger the same way.

If you're upgrading from a tank to tankless, expect a slightly different experience at the tap. Tankless units deliver a steady temperature rather than the gradual cooling of a draining tank, but they do have a minimum flow rate to activate, so a trickle from a single fixture may not trigger heating the way a full shower does.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need a permit to replace my water heater in Salem?

Yes. The City of Salem requires a permit for water heater replacements, and we handle that process as part of the installation.

How long does a replacement take?

Most tank-to-tank replacements are completed in a few hours. A switch to tankless can take longer if venting or gas line upgrades are needed.

Do you haul away the old unit?

Yes, removal and disposal of the old tank is included in every replacement.

Is switching to tankless worth the extra cost?

It depends on your household's hot water use and how long you plan to stay in the home. We'll walk through the real numbers for your situation rather than a one-size answer.
