Distinct From Repiping

The Service Line, Not the Pipes Inside Your Home

A water line is the supply pipe running from the water meter or curb stop to your house, separate from the interior plumbing our repiping team handles. A break or leak here shows up as low pressure sitewide, a wet patch in the yard between the street and the house, or an unusually high water bill even when nothing inside the home has changed.

Local Cause

Why Water Lines Fail in Salem

Salem's volcanic clay soil expands and contracts with the seasons the same way it stresses sewer laterals, and that movement shifts buried water lines enough to crack fittings or separate joints over time. Older galvanized service lines, common in homes built before the 1980s, also corrode internally and can develop pinhole leaks independent of any soil movement at all.

Signs

Signs You Have a Water Line Problem

Low Pressure Throughout the House

Every fixture affected at once points to the service line, not one fixture.

Wet Patch Between Street and House

Standing water or unusually green grass along the line's path.

Unexplained Bill Increase

A meter that keeps running even with the house shut off points to a line leak.

Discolored Water Sitewide

Rust-colored water from every fixture can mean a corroding galvanized service line.

Low Pressure Everywhere?

We can usually diagnose a water line problem without full excavation.

Repair Options

Trenchless Replacement vs. Open-Cut

1

Locate

Confirm the leak's location along the line's path.

2

Choose Method

Trenchless replacement or open-cut, based on access and damage.

3

Replace or Repair

Install new line or fix the specific damaged section.

4

Test & Restore

Pressure test the line, then restore the surface above it.

Trenchless water line replacement pulls a new line through the path of the old one with minimal surface disruption, which works well when the ground above is otherwise undisturbed. Open-cut work is sometimes the more practical choice when other utilities run near the line or access is limited. All repair and replacement work follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Diagnosis

Finding the Leak Before Digging

Rather than guessing where along the line a leak might be, we use acoustic and pressure-testing equipment to narrow down the location first. That precision matters on a longer service line, since excavating the wrong section wastes time and money on a repair that isn't needed. Once we've confirmed the location and extent of the damage, we'll walk through whether a targeted repair or a full line replacement is the more honest recommendation for your situation.

FAQs

Common Questions

How is a water line different from the pipes inside my house?

The water line runs from the meter or curb stop to your home. Interior plumbing after that point is a separate system, which our repiping team handles if it needs replacement.

Will you have to dig up my whole yard?

Not necessarily. Trenchless replacement handles many situations without full excavation, depending on access and what's along the line's path.

How do I know if it's a water line leak or a leak inside my house?

Low pressure or discoloration at every fixture, along with a wet patch in the yard, points to the service line. An issue at just one fixture usually isn't.

Do I need a permit for water line replacement?

Yes, and we handle the permitting and inspection as part of the project.
