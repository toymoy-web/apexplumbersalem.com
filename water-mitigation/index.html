Distinct From Restoration

Mitigation Is the First 24 to 48 Hours

Water mitigation and water damage restoration are two phases of the same event, not the same service. Mitigation is the urgent, first-response work: extracting standing water, drying out saturated materials, and containing the affected area to stop damage from spreading further. Our water damage restoration service covers what comes after, the fuller repair and rebuild of damaged flooring, drywall, and structural materials once the property is stable and dry.

Knowing which phase you actually need helps set expectations from the first call, rather than assuming every water event requires a full reconstruction project.

Our Process

What Mitigation Involves

1

Stop the Spread

Contain the affected area to prevent water from reaching unaffected spaces.

2

Extract

Remove standing water with pumps and extraction equipment.

3

Dry

Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers begin drying saturated materials.

4

Hand Off or Continue

Stabilize the property, then move into full restoration if needed.

Our process follows IICRC S500 water damage mitigation guidelines, the published industry standard covering initial response through structural drying. Not every water event needs full restoration afterward, sometimes mitigation alone, extraction and drying, is enough to prevent lasting damage if we're called quickly.

Local Cause

Why Fast Mitigation Matters in Salem

Marion County's roughly 40 inches of annual rain, concentrated between October and March, means groundwater and storm-driven water events are a real seasonal risk here. Salem's volcanic clay soil movement also stresses buried plumbing lines, and a burst pipe or sewer backup can introduce a large volume of water into a home very quickly. The faster mitigation starts after any of these events, the less likely the situation progresses into a full restoration project.

Documentation

What to Document Before We Arrive

If it's safe to do so, take photos or video of the affected area before mitigation work begins. This is useful if you file an insurance claim, since it captures the extent of the event before extraction and drying change what's visible. We're not a substitute for your insurance adjuster, but a clear record from the start tends to make that process smoother.

Scope of Work

What's Included in Mitigation

Mitigation covers standing water extraction, structural drying with air movers and dehumidifiers, and moisture monitoring until materials reach a safe level. It does not include replacing damaged flooring, drywall, or cabinetry, that scope falls under restoration once the property is stable. Some properties need both services back to back, while others, when mitigation starts quickly enough, avoid needing restoration at all.

FAQs

Common Questions

What's the difference between mitigation and restoration?

Mitigation is the urgent first response: extraction, drying, and containment to stop damage from spreading. Restoration is the fuller repair and rebuild of damaged materials afterward. Some events only need mitigation.

How fast do you respond to a water mitigation call?

We treat active water events as emergencies and dispatch as quickly as we can, since the first 24 to 48 hours matter most for preventing mold growth and further damage.

Will I definitely need full restoration after mitigation?

Not always. If mitigation starts quickly enough, some properties only need extraction and drying without further reconstruction work.

Do you document damage for insurance purposes?

Yes, we document thoroughly during mitigation, which supports whatever claim process follows.

What equipment do you use for drying?

Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers, along with moisture meters to monitor progress until materials reach a safe level.
